# Formal evidence

Read this reference before endorsing or challenging a derivation, proof, analytic approximation, or equation-level claim.

## Close the relevant dependency chain

- Inspect the definitions, assumptions, quantifiers, conventions, boundary conditions, and appendix or supplement spans that control the result before declaring a step absent.
- Reproduce enough of the argument to distinguish an error from omitted exposition. Check applicable signs, factors, dimensions, normalization, limiting cases, symmetries, and substitution back into the defining equations.
- Identify where an approximation enters and whether its stated regime controls the neglected terms. Do not promote agreement in one limit into a general proof.
- Test a claimed implication against the weakest assumptions actually stated. A stronger unstated premise is an evidence gap even when the conclusion may be correct.

## Calibrate the finding

Distinguish a verified error, an incomplete argument, a likely issue whose controlling premise is unavailable, a limitation, and material independently checked and found consistent. Describe severity by impact on the claim and confidence by certainty of the diagnosis; do not collapse them into one score.

State which steps, limits, or substitutions were actually checked. Symbolic or numerical spot checks support only the identities and regimes they evaluate.
